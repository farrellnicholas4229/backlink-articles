# Aluminum Window Cost Per M2: The Metric That Exposes Overpriced Quotes

## Why Cost Per M2 Is the Only Number That Makes Window Quotes Comparable

A quote that says $1,400 per window sounds useful until the next quote uses a different mix of sizes. A 0.8 m² bathroom awning and a 4.0 m² living-room slider should never be judged by the same dollar amount. One opening can be small but hardware-heavy; the other can be large but simple. Per-window pricing counts units. Cost per m2 measures value.

That matters because window quotes are usually a blend of very different products. Fixed panels, casements, sliders, and bi-folds do not carry the same fabrication burden, so the headline number alone can hide a lot of distortion. On a mixed project, the only way to compare suppliers honestly is to reduce every quote to the same unit of measure.

## What the Number Actually Measures

The calculation is simple, but the measurement has to be consistent. Width times height gives area, and the total quote divided by total area gives the cost per m2. The important part is using the same rule for every supplier. Frame-to-frame dimensions, not visible glass size, are the only sensible basis for comparison across quotes.

If one supplier measures only the glass and another measures the full external frame, the numbers may look precise while being completely incompatible. That is how a quote can appear cheaper on paper even when it is not cheaper in reality. A 10 percent measurement difference can turn into a false bargain or a fake premium before the first window is installed.

## How the Same Quote Can Hide Opposite Realities

A small opening often carries a higher cost per m2 than a large one because fixed overheads get spread over less area. Hardware, fabrication setup, and minimum charge thresholds do not shrink just because the opening is small.

Two examples make the problem obvious:

- A 900 mm x 900 mm bathroom awning is 0.81 m². A $900 quote works out to $1,111 per m2.
- A 2.4 m x 1.8 m living-room slider is 4.32 m². A $2,000 quote works out to $463 per m2.

The smaller window costs less in total, but it is far more expensive per unit area. That is why a cheap-looking per-window quote can be the worst value in the entire schedule once the size mix is normalized.

## Where Overpriced Quotes Hide

A proper [cost per m2 breakdown](https://meichenwindows.com.au/aluminium-windows-cost-per-m2/) only works when every supplier is quoting the same scope, the same spec, and the same measuring method. When those three things drift, the number stops being a comparison tool and becomes a disguise.

The most common distortions show up in predictable places:

- supply-only pricing compared against supply-and-install pricing
- flashings, sealants, delivery, or disposal left out of one quote
- different glazing levels, such as single versus double glazing
- different frame systems, especially thermally broken versus non-thermally broken profiles
- different area definitions, such as glass area versus frame area

The low quote is not always the better quote. Often it is just the quote with the most missing pieces.

## How to Normalize Quotes So the Number Means Something

The only reliable way to compare suppliers is to force every number into the same structure before judging it.

1. List every opening with width and height in meters.
2. Multiply width by height to get the area of each opening.
3. Add the areas together to get the total project area.
4. Divide the total supply quote by total area.
5. If installation is itemized, divide the install total by total area separately.
6. Compare only quotes that use the same frame, glazing, finish, and scope.

That last step matters more than most buyers think. A double-glazed thermally broken system and a basic single-glazed system can both be priced per m2, but the lower number is meaningless unless the systems are equivalent. Same with hardware, powder coat, and warranty coverage. The metric is useful only after the specification is aligned.

## What a Fair Comparison Looks Like

A fair quote makes the answer obvious. It tells you exactly how much area is being priced, what is included in the supplied unit, what installation covers, and what has been excluded. Once those variables are locked down, the cost per m2 becomes the cleanest way to spot a markup, a bargain, or a stripped-down scope.

That is why the best buyers do not ask only what a window costs. They ask what the same opening costs per square meter under the same conditions. If three suppliers quote $680 per m2, $720 per m2, and $510 per m2, the lowest number is not automatically the best number. It is only the best number if the scope matches and nothing important has been pushed out of sight.

When the scope is honest, the math is honest. When the scope is incomplete, the headline price is just a teaser.
