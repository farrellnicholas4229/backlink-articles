# Aluminium Window Quotes in Melbourne: Why the Cheapest Price Usually Costs More

## The number at the bottom is usually missing a job

If you're comparing [aluminium window prices](https://meichenwindows.com.au/aluminium-windows-melbourne-prices/), the first mistake is treating every quote as if it covers the same work. A window price is not a finished-project price. It may cover only the product, only the product plus installation, or a full replacement that includes removal, disposal, and making good. Those are three different jobs wearing the same label.

In practice, the cheapest quote is often the smallest scope, not the best value. The difference shows up later, when the builder, plasterer, painter, or disposal contractor gets added back in.

## Why the same opening can be quoted three different ways

A 1200 mm by 1200 mm sliding window can be priced as:

- supply only
- supply and retrofit install
- full-frame replacement with finishing

Each version changes the labor, materials, and risk.

Supply only is exactly that: a frame, glass, hardware, and nothing else. It works if you already have an installer lined up and the existing opening is clean, square, and ready.

Retrofit installation drops the new window into the old frame or opening. That can look cheaper because it avoids demolition, but it also leaves the original frame in place. If the old frame is warped, rotten, corroded, or badly out of square, the cheaper method can become a compromise rather than a solution.

Full-frame replacement is the real renovation job. The old unit comes out, the opening is adjusted if needed, the new window is fixed, sealed, and finished, and the wall is made presentable again. That takes more time, but it is the only option that actually resets the opening.

## The hidden line items that change the total

Most budget blowouts come from items that never look dramatic on their own:

- removal of old windows
- rubbish hauling
- scaffold or height access
- trim, sealant, and flashing
- plaster patching
- render repair
- paint touch-up
- permits or inspections
- upgraded glass for compliance
- specialty hardware or finishes

A quote that skips these items is not necessarily dishonest. It may simply be incomplete. The problem is that homeowners often compare the headline figure as if it already includes everything needed to finish the wall. It usually does not.

That is why one quote can look 30 percent cheaper and still end up more expensive after the extras are added. A reliable [Melbourne pricing breakdown](https://meichenwindows.com.au/aluminium-windows-melbourne-prices/) should make the scope visible before the contract is signed, not after the invoice lands.

## A real example from a standard renovation

Picture a 1960s brick veneer home replacing four aging windows.

Quote A:

- supply only
- basic single glazing
- no removal
- no finishing

Headline price: $3,900

Quote B:

- supply and install
- removal included
- standard finishing
- no plaster or paint

Headline price: $5,800

Quote C:

- full-frame replacement
- removal and disposal
- new flashing and sealants
- internal and external making good
- double glazing

Headline price: $7,200

At first glance, Quote A looks like the bargain. In reality, it still needs an installer, a disposal run, and finish work. By the time those costs are added, the real total can surpass Quote B. If the opening is out of square or the old frame is damaged, Quote A can also create more labor for everyone downstream.

The cheapest quote only wins if it already includes the work required to complete the job to the standard you expect.

## What a useful quote must say plainly

A serious quote should answer these questions without a follow-up email:

1. Is it supply only, retrofit, or full-frame replacement?
2. Which exact window series or frame profile is being priced?
3. What glass specification is included?
4. Are removal and disposal included?
5. Is making good included, and to what extent?
6. Are seals, trims, and flashing included?
7. Are access costs, such as scaffolding, included?
8. What is excluded?
9. How long is the quote valid?
10. What warranty applies to product and installation?

If any of those answers are vague, the quote is not really comparable to the others. It may still be a fair price for the scope being offered, but it is not a complete basis for comparison.

## Why low quotes are often incomplete quotes

There are a few common reasons a window quote comes in far below the rest:

- the supplier quoted supply only
- the installer assumed easy access that your site does not have
- the quote left out finishing trades
- the glass spec is lower than the others
- compliance upgrades were ignored
- the framing method is a retrofit, not a full replacement

That last point matters most. Two quotes can describe the same opening while actually pricing different construction methods. One is preserving old material. The other is replacing it. Those are not interchangeable.

## The safest way to compare quotes

The goal is not to find the cheapest number. It is to normalize the scope so every supplier is pricing the same job.

Ask each supplier to quote the same:

- dimensions
- opening type
- glass performance
- frame finish
- install method
- removal scope
- finishing scope

Then add the missing pieces yourself if one quote excludes them. Once every option is brought to the same scope, the real differences become obvious. You may still choose the more expensive quote, but now the reason is visible: better glass, better warranty, cleaner finishing, or a method that solves the actual problem instead of hiding it.

That is the difference between buying a window and buying a completed opening.

The homeowners who avoid budget shock usually do one thing differently: they treat every quote as a scope document first and a price second. The number matters, but only after the work behind the number has been identified.
